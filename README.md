# GlnLimUncouplingOmics
Transcriptome and proteome responses are uncoupled under glutamine limitation

## Abstract
Understanding the quantitative relationship between transcript and protein concentrations has significant implications for biomedical research and biotechnology. Previous studies of transcript-protein relationships in human cells are limited by the use of relative rather than absolute quantitation, and the presence of confounding factors. Here, we address these limitations by performing absolute quantitation of both transcripts and proteins in cells upon perturbation of glutamine availability. Glutamine is an important nutrient that supplies carbon and nitrogen to fuel biosynthesis and is usually present at high concentrations in culture media.  By reducing extracellular glutamine from the full media to near- and below-physiological concentrations, we find that the transcriptome and proteome responses are uncoupled under glutamine limitation. The transcriptome response consists of both upregulated and downregulated transcripts, with a near-constant total transcript concentration across all glutamine concentrations. In contrast, the proteome response is characterized by uniform changes for all proteins, leading to large differences in total protein concentration and minimal differences in the relative concentrations of individual proteins. Genes with high within-gene correlations are enriched in specific functional groups such as ribosome biogenesis and translation machinery, while genes with low within-gene correlations are enriched in actin cytoskeleton organization and vesicle and membrane trafficking. Further analyses showed that 26 gene features were significantly associated with the degree of within-gene correlation, with the 3’ UTR length showing the largest effect. Our work sheds light on the distinct factors associated with gene expression regulation related to gene function and gene feature, which has broad implications for medicine and biotechnology.
